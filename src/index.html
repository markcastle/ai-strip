Every image carries data you can't see: where it was taken, which camera took it, and whether an AI made it. Trace shows you, then removes it.

Your photo never leaves this device. Trace makes no network requests and works with the internet switched off.

What Trace removes. The hidden data stored alongside the picture: GPS coordinates, camera and lens serial numbers, timestamps, editing history, generation prompts, and C2PA Content Credentials. The picture itself is copied across byte for byte, so cleaning costs you no image quality, however many times you do it.

What Trace cannot remove. Some AI images also carry an invisible watermark such as SynthID, which is part of the pixels rather than the file's data. Cropping, filters, screenshots and this tool all leave it intact, and no tool that works on metadata can detect or remove it. Trace will never tell you an image is untraceable, because it can't know that.

To check for a watermark you need the vendor's own verifier, such as openai.com/verify. Trace reads Content Credentials but does not verify their signatures; for that, use the Content Authenticity Initiative's c2pa-web library.
